01 / Desktop design challenge

Make technical trade-offs visible.

A two-to-three-period performance task asks students to design a practical $500 desktop computer and an unrestricted high-performance gaming system. Both builds require realistic components and a defensible compatibility analysis.

Scenario 01

Practical computer

Meet a strict budget and justify compromises for everyday use.

Scenario 02

High-performance system

Evaluate component choices, cooling, power and performance without a fixed price ceiling.

The finished student packet includes activation questions, component research, a spreadsheet specification with conditional formatting, a five-minute presentation and a rubric. The task evaluates reasoning and compatibility rather than memorization alone.

02 / Python looping unit

Connect explanation, prediction and independent coding.

The completed student notes and six coordinated lesson presentations cover while loops, for loops, break and continue, nested control structures, input validation and dictionary lookups. The materials connect concepts to trace tables, optional practice and assigned coursework.

01Activate and explain

Use a short example and learning objective to introduce the concept.

02Predict and trace

Record expected output and follow changes to variables before running code.

03Apply independently

Complete practice and transition to official CS50P assignments with their own instructions and checks.

CodeHS and Harvard CS50P are referenced as external instructional resources; their content is not republished here or represented as my own work.
